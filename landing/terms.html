1. What DraftGem is

DraftGem is a game of skill built on the Solana blockchain. In each tournament you pick five coins within a budget of 4,000 Fantasy Points (FP). A coin's price in FP reflects how much it typically moves. When the round ends, each coin's score is the percentage change of its price over the round (never lower than -100% for a single coin), and your portfolio's score is the sum of its five coins. Scores follow from recorded market prices, not from any random draw.

The game is currently in beta. Features, limits, fees and availability may change.

4. Tournaments, entry fees and prizes

  • Entering. You pay the tournament's entry fee, in SKR, SOL, ORE or USDC, in a transaction you approve in your wallet. Entries lock when the round starts and cannot be changed or withdrawn after that.
  • Where the money is. Entry fees go into a vault controlled by the on-chain program, which pays prizes out and can refund entries. We do not hold the pool in a wallet of our own.
  • Prizes. Depending on the tournament, prizes go to the top half of players, Top 1, Top 3, the top 30%, or the winner of a head-to-head duel. Players who tie share their combined prize. Prizes are paid to the wallet that entered.
  • Fees. The platform keeps 5% of each pool. In a tournament created by a player, the creator receives a further 5%.
  • Cancelled tournaments. If a tournament is cancelled, entry fees can be refunded to the players through the program.
  • Held payouts. Before paying out, our system checks the numbers. If something does not add up, the payout is held until it is resolved rather than sent.
  • Beta limits. While DraftGem is in beta, entry fees are capped at small amounts for each currency.
  • Network fees. You pay the Solana network fees for your own transactions.

5. Prices and results

Coin prices come from public market data providers. The platform records each coin's start and end price on the blockchain, and the program computes scores from them. The platform's authority key is trusted to register prices and the prize plan, and our system checks them before paying out. Market data can be wrong, delayed or unavailable, and thinly traded coins can move sharply. We do not guarantee the accuracy of any price, score or ranking, and we may delay, hold or cancel a tournament if we find a problem.

A "private" tournament is only unlisted: anyone with the link can find it, and its blockchain data is public.
